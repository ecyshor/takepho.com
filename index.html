Every trip you’ve taken, found for you

The Photos timeline reads your Takeout and works out where you lived, when you travelled and what you saw. Browse it as a timeline (rows, or one long scroll by year), on a map, trip by trip or as numbers.

All your trips on one map

  • Holidays, day trips and home periods are told apart automatically, from your photo locations
  • Each trip shows its places joined in the order you visited them, with towns and countries named from the GPS coordinates
  • Add your Google Maps Timeline (Location History, with Lifetime or Yearly) and trips start and end when you really left and came home; photos without GPS get a location, and Travel shows your flights and long journeys

Each trip, day by day

  • A day-by-day page per trip: where you were and what you photographed
  • On-device AI picks the best photos and a cover for each trip, and stacks near-duplicates
  • Your Strava, Komoot or Garmin activities appear on the day they happened
  • Open any photo to see the full-resolution original, read straight from the archive

Your travels in numbers

  • Countries visited and holiday days per year, split by country
  • Flights taken and kilometres flown, once you add your Google Maps Timeline
  • Holidays by country, and where you lived when

Make it yours

Rename and annotate. Rename a trip, add notes, pick its cover, hide trips or photos you do not want to see. (Lifetime and Yearly)
Fix your homes. Set or edit where you lived and re-run trip detection. (Lifetime and Yearly)
Add your partner’s photos. With Yearly, add someone else’s Takeout: their photos join the trips you shared, marked with their colour.
Deleted in Google Photos. Photos that disappeared from your newest export are listed so you can keep or remove them. (Lifetime and Yearly)
Your edits survive. Import a newer export and trip names, notes, homes and hidden photos are kept.

Your photos on your Strava, Komoot and Garmin routes

The Activities app matches the photos in your Takeout to your activities by capture time and puts each one on the GPS track.

Every photo placed along the route

  • Each photo sits on the track with its distance and time; photos without GPS are placed by time
  • Best photos, a photo strip and an elevation profile with a marker for every photo
  • Splits and a link back to the activity on Strava
  • Photos you already uploaded to Strava are shown too, with duplicates hidden

One map of everything you did

  • Every route on one map, coloured by sport, with a heatmap mode
  • Filter by sport, year, country, duration or “with photos”
  • A Stats page totals distance, moving time and elevation per year and per sport
  • The link goes both ways: activities appear inside your trips, and each activity links to its trip

Connecting your accounts

Strava. Official API, through your own free Strava API app; a step-by-step guide is built in.
Komoot and Garmin Connect. Sign in with your own login; activities and tracks stay on your computer.
No duplicates. The same activity recorded on two services is shown once.

Move your Google Photos to Proton Photos

The sync app uploads the Google Photos part of your Takeout to Proton Photos in full original quality, with the dates, places and albums intact.

Albums that make sense

  • Keep the albums as they are in your Takeout, or get one album per detected trip
  • Capture date, GPS, description, favourite and people are written from the sidecars into the files first, without re-encoding the pixels
  • Files without a capture date are flagged, with one-click date suggestions
  • Full original quality uploads through Proton's official Drive CLI

A sync you can stop, resume and undo

Pause and resume. Progress and time remaining are shown; an interrupted sync continues on the next start.
Nothing is deleted. Re-runs skip what is already in Proton, and a sync never deletes anything in your Proton account.
Change your mind. Revert sync moves what the app uploaded to the Proton trash.
Only your own photos. Other people’s Takeouts that you added never go to Proton.

On your computer, not in the cloud:

  • Interrupted imports resume where they left off

Frequently asked questions

Is it a web app?

No. It is a desktop app for Mac and Windows, and there is a Docker image you can run on a NAS. Your photos stay on your computer unless you choose to sync them to Proton Photos.

Is there a phone app?

No. Takeout Photo is a desktop app for Mac and Windows. If you run the Docker image on a NAS or home server, you can open it in the browser of any device on your network, including a phone.

Which computers does it run on?

Mac: Apple silicon (arm64), macOS 12 (Monterey) or later; signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Windows: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.

How much disk space does it need?

The archives themselves, plus a small local library. For a real 249 GB Takeout with about 34,000 photos the app stores about 12.6 GB of thumbnails and previews, a 370 MB index and a 170 MB database. The archives are read in place and never unpacked or copied.

Do I need to keep the Takeout archives after importing?

Yes, if you want full-resolution originals. Browsing uses local previews, but originals are read from the archives on demand, so keep them in your folder or on Drive.

How does the app update?

Both apps update themselves. Mac: use "Check for Updates…" in the app menu, or let it check automatically (you can turn automatic checks off in Settings → Updates). Windows: the installed app checks for updates daily and installs them when the app quits (toggle in Settings → Updates, plus a "Check for updates now" button); while the exe isn't code-signed, updates are verified with an EdDSA signature. The portable zip doesn't update itself — download new versions from the releases page. Updates are free for everyone, no plan needed. Release notes on GitHub.

Why does Windows show "Windows protected your PC" when I open the app?

The Windows app isn't code-signed yet, so on the first launch Windows shows that warning. Click "More info", then "Run anyway" — the app is safe to run. The installed app updates itself automatically (checks daily, Settings → Updates; installs when the app quits); the portable zip doesn't — new versions are on the releases page.

Do I need to pay?

No, not for Lite: it is free for your 5 most recent holidays (photo timeline only, no account needed). A paid plan (Lifetime or Yearly) unlocks the full library with no photo limits and the other apps (activity matching, Proton Photos move).

What is Lite?

Lite is the free plan: no account or licence needed. Import your own Google Takeout manually (a local folder of .tgz/.zip archives, including a newer export later) and get the photo timeline only, read-only, limited to your 5 most recent holidays plus the home periods and day trips in that span (the app shows “5 of N holidays”). Activity matching (Strava/Komoot/Garmin) and the Proton Photos move stay on the paid plans. Buying Lifetime or Yearly unlocks the whole library: the rest of the photos are processed in the background, without re-importing.

Can I rename or hide trips?

Yes, with Lifetime or Yearly: rename a trip, add notes, pick its cover, hide trips or photos, and set your homes. Lite is read-only. Your edits are kept when you import a newer export.

Do I need an account?

No, not for Lite (see “What is Lite?”). The app is also free to download and explore (the sample library in the app, or the web demo). A paid plan needs an account: sign in with Google or Apple from the app (Settings → Account). One account covers 2 devices (Mac or Windows).

Does it work offline?

Yes. The app keeps a signed entitlement on your computer and refreshes it about weekly, so it needs internet at least once a month.

What happens if an import is interrupted?

Interrupted work resumes automatically: if the app is closed, the computer restarts or the app updates mid-import or mid-processing, it picks up where it left off on the next start. Already imported items are skipped, an import that stopped has a “Resume import” button, and every import shows its steps as a checklist with the current step live.

Does it need my Google password?

No. Sign-in happens on Google's own page, and you can revoke access any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What about the Google Photos API?

Google no longer lets apps read a whole Photos library, so Google Takeout is the way to get all your photos with their metadata. Takeout exports include everything you need.

Does it show videos and HEIC photos?

HEIC photos are decoded on your computer and shown like any other photo. The timeline shows photos today; videos in your Takeout are counted in the inventory and uploaded by the Proton Photos sync.

Why are the dates wrong after unzipping a Google Takeout?

The file's own timestamp is often the export date; the real capture date and GPS live in the .json sidecar next to each photo. Takeout Photo reads those sidecars, so dates and places are right. See browsing a Takeout as a timeline.

Can I see my Google Photos on my Strava, Komoot or Garmin activities?

Yes. The Activities app matches your Google Photos from the Takeout to each activity by capture time and places every photo along the GPS track ("km 12.3 · 10:42"). See photos on your Strava activities — Komoot and Garmin Connect work the same way.

How do I move my Google Photos to Proton Photos?

The sync app moves the Google Photos part of your Takeout into Proton Photos in full original quality, keeping albums (or making trip albums) and writing dates, GPS and descriptions from the JSON sidecars into the files first. See moving Google Photos to Proton Photos.

Does it work with Google Drive or Docs from my Takeout?

Not yet. Today it uses the Google Photos part of your Takeout, plus Location History where your export has it. Support for Google Drive and Google Docs files is coming, and the sync app will add more destinations beyond Proton Photos.

Can I add someone else's Takeout, like my partner's?

Yes. With the yearly plan you can add contributors (other people's Takeouts, e.g. a partner's), so their photos attach to the trips you shared.

What happens when the yearly plan ends?

You keep the Lifetime licence (the first paid year includes it). Manual imports keep working forever, and importing a newer export still rebuilds your library with your edits kept. The automatic-processing features (folder watcher, incremental imports of newer exports, new contributors) stop while the subscription is inactive; existing contributors stay visible. You can re-subscribe any time.

Does the yearly price go up?

No — it goes down. The first year is billed at the full price (e.g. USD 25), and every renewal after that is automatically 50% off (USD 12.50 per year; EUR 20 → EUR 10, GBP 20 → GBP 10, CHF 20 → CHF 10). Nothing for you to do; it renews at half price until you cancel.

How do I cancel or get a refund?

Cancel any time from the app's "Manage subscription" button; the plan runs to the end of the paid year. For a refund, email hello@takepho.com and we will sort it out.
