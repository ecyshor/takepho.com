Find every trip in your photos, and keep each one as an album.

Takeout Photo reads your Google Photos Takeout and, on a Mac, your Apple Photos library. It finds your holidays and day trips, shows each one on a map and day by day, and turns it into an album in Apple Photos and Proton Photos, copying your Google photos across. It all runs on your computer.

1

Bring in your Google and Apple photos

Start with your Google Photos Takeout, your Apple Photos library, or both. They become one library, read and processed on your computer.

Google Photos, from a Takeout

  • Export your photos at takeout.google.com and point the app at the folder of archives, a Google Drive link, a Proton Drive folder, or sign in with Google and pick the files (read-only)
  • Archives are read where they are, never unpacked or copied, even at hundreds of GB
  • Real capture dates, places and descriptions come from the JSON files Google puts next to each photo
  • A newer export merges in, and your trip names, notes and hidden photos are kept

Apple Photos, on your Mac

  • Give the app access to your Photos library once, and your Apple photos join the same timeline
  • Photos kept in iCloud are shown from their previews; originals download only if you allow it
  • New photos come in with Check for new photos on Lifetime, or by themselves on Yearly
  • Hidden photos stay out unless you include them
2

Explore your life one trip at a time

The app works out where you lived, when you went away and where you went. Every holiday and day trip gets its own page, its places and its route on the map.

All your trips on one map

  • Holidays, day trips and time at home are told apart from where your photos were taken
  • Each trip’s places are joined in the order you visited them, with towns and countries named
  • Add your Google Maps Timeline and trips start and end when you really left and came home; photos without GPS get a place, and your flights show up too

Each trip, day by day

  • Where you were each day and what you photographed
  • On-device AI picks the best photos and a cover, and stacks near-duplicates
  • Open any photo in full resolution, straight from the archive or your Apple library
  • Rename a trip, add notes, pick its cover or hide what you don’t want to see (Lifetime and Yearly)

Your rides and hikes inside the trip

  • Connect Strava, Komoot or Garmin Connect and your photos are placed along each activity’s GPS track
  • Activities appear on the day they happened in the trip, and every activity links back to its trip
  • One map of every route, with a heatmap and yearly stats

More about photos on your Strava, Komoot and Garmin activities

  • Your travels in numbers. Countries, holiday days per year, flights and kilometres flown, on the overview page.
  • Fix your homes. Set where you lived and when you moved, and trips are detected again.
  • Add your partner’s photos. On Yearly, another person’s Takeout joins the trips you shared, in their own colour.

Turn your trips into albums in Apple Photos and Proton Photos

Choose how your Google photos are organized once, and both destinations follow it. Your trips become albums you can open on your phone, in Photos or in Proton.

As in your Takeout

Your Google albums stay albums. Everything else goes to the timeline, by date.

Trip albums

One album per detected trip, named like “Holiday Lisbon, Portugal – August 2024”. Pick holidays, day trips or both, and keep trip photos in their Google albums too if you like. Photos outside trips still go across, to the timeline and their Google albums.

  • No second copies in Apple Photos. A Google photo that is already in your Apple library is added to the trip album as it is, instead of being imported again.
  • Favourites carry over. Photos you starred in Google Photos are favourites in Apple Photos too; Proton gets the favourite flag in the file.
  • Only your own photos. Another person’s Takeout that you added is used for the shared trips, never copied into your Apple or Proton account.
4

Back up your Google photos where you keep the rest

The same run that builds the albums copies your Google photos across in full original quality. You can stop it at any point, and undo it.

Apple Photos on your Mac, and iCloud Photos if you use it

  • A report before anything changes. The app first checks your Takeout against your Apple library and shows what is already there, what it would import, and the few it is not sure about, side by side for you to decide.
  • Only what is missing is imported. Live Photos and RAW+JPEG pairs arrive as one photo, videos as videos.
  • Stops before your disk fills. The import pauses with a clear message when the library’s disk runs low.
  • Undo everything. One button moves what the app imported to Recently Deleted (kept 30 days) and removes the albums and favourites it added.

Proton Photos end-to-end encrypted, in Proton’s cloud

  • Full original quality. Uploads go through Proton’s official Drive CLI.
  • Dates and places inside the files. Capture date, GPS, description and favourite are written into each file first, without re-encoding the pixels.
  • Nothing is deleted. Re-runs skip what is already in Proton, and a sync never deletes anything in your account.
  • Revert sync. Moves what the app uploaded to the Proton trash.

More about moving Google Photos to Proton Photos

  • Pause and resume. Progress and time left are shown; an interrupted run carries on at the next start.
  • After every new export. Turn on automatic sync and new Google photos go across after each import.
  • Files without a date are flagged before they go anywhere, with one-click date suggestions.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a web app?

No. It is a desktop app for Mac and Windows, and there is a Docker image you can run on a NAS. Your photos stay on your computer unless you choose to sync them to Proton Photos or Apple Photos.

Is there a phone app?

No. Takeout Photo is a desktop app for Mac and Windows. The trip albums it builds in Apple Photos and Proton Photos are on your phone through those apps. If you run the Docker image on a NAS or home server, you can open it in the browser of any device on your network, including a phone.

Can it import my Apple Photos library?

Yes, in the Mac app. Give it access to your Photos library and your Apple photos join the same timeline as your Takeout, with each photo that is in both shown once. Photos stored in iCloud are shown from their previews, and originals download only if you allow it. Lite imports your 500 most recent Apple photos once; Lifetime imports the whole library and has a “Check for new photos” button; Yearly picks up new photos by itself. The Windows app and the Docker image do not read Apple Photos.

Can I organize my trips into albums in Apple Photos or Proton Photos?

Yes, with Lifetime or Yearly. Choose “Trip albums” and every detected holiday or day trip becomes an album, or keep the albums as they are in your Takeout. The same layout is used in Proton Photos and, on the Mac, in Apple Photos. The albums are built from the Google photos in your Takeout.

Will copying my Google photos into Apple Photos create duplicates?

No. The app first checks every Google photo against your Apple library and shows a report: what is already there, what it would import, and the few it is not sure about, side by side for you to decide. Only the missing photos are imported; a photo that is already there gets the Google albums and favourite added instead. Nothing is written before you confirm, and “Undo everything this app did in Apple Photos” moves the imported photos to Recently Deleted.

How do I move my Google Photos to Proton Photos?

The sync app moves the Google Photos part of your Takeout into Proton Photos in full original quality, keeping albums (or making trip albums) and writing dates, GPS and descriptions from the JSON sidecars into the files first. See moving Google Photos to Proton Photos.

Which computers does it run on?

Mac: Apple silicon (arm64), macOS 12 (Monterey) or later; signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Windows: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.

How much disk space does it need?

The archives themselves, plus a small local library. For a real 249 GB Takeout with about 34,000 photos the app stores about 12.6 GB of thumbnails and previews, a 370 MB index and a 170 MB database. The archives are read in place and never unpacked or copied.

Do I need to keep the Takeout archives after importing?

Yes, if you want full-resolution originals. Browsing uses local previews, but originals are read from the archives on demand, so keep them in your folder or on Drive.

How does the app update?

Both apps update themselves. Mac: use "Check for Updates…" in the app menu, or let it check automatically (you can turn automatic checks off in Settings → Updates). Windows: the installed app checks for updates daily and installs them when the app quits (toggle in Settings → Updates, plus a "Check for updates now" button); while the exe isn't code-signed, updates are verified with an EdDSA signature. The portable zip doesn't update itself — download new versions from the releases page. Updates are free for everyone, no plan needed. Release notes on GitHub.

Why does Windows show "Windows protected your PC" when I open the app?

The Windows app isn't code-signed yet, so on the first launch Windows shows that warning. Click "More info", then "Run anyway" — the app is safe to run. The installed app updates itself automatically (checks daily, Settings → Updates; installs when the app quits); the portable zip doesn't — new versions are on the releases page.

Do I need to pay?

No, not for Lite: it is free for your trips of the last year (at least your 5 most recent holidays; photo timeline only, no account needed). A paid plan (Lifetime or Yearly) unlocks the full library with no photo limits, the trip albums and backups in Apple Photos and Proton Photos, and activity matching.

What is Lite?

Lite is the free plan: no account or licence needed. Import your own Google Takeout manually (a local folder of .tgz/.zip archives, including a newer export later) and, on a Mac, your 500 most recent Apple photos once, and get the photo timeline only, read-only, limited to your trips of the last year (at least your 5 most recent holidays) plus the home periods and day trips in that span. Albums and backups in Apple Photos and Proton Photos, and activity matching (Strava/Komoot/Garmin), stay on the paid plans. Buying Lifetime or Yearly unlocks the whole library: the rest of the photos are processed in the background, without re-importing.

Can I rename or hide trips?

Yes, with Lifetime or Yearly: rename a trip, add notes, pick its cover, hide trips or photos, and set your homes. Lite is read-only. Your edits are kept when you import a newer export.

Do I need an account?

No, not for Lite (see “What is Lite?”). The app is also free to download and explore (the sample library in the app, or the web demo). A paid plan needs an account: sign in with Google or Apple from the app (Settings → Account). One account covers 2 devices (Mac or Windows).

Does it work offline?

Yes. The app keeps a signed entitlement on your computer and refreshes it about weekly, so it needs internet at least once a month.

What happens if an import is interrupted?

Interrupted work resumes automatically: if the app is closed, the computer restarts or the app updates mid-import or mid-processing, it picks up where it left off on the next start. Already imported items are skipped, an import that stopped has a “Resume import” button, and every import shows its steps as a checklist with the current step live.

Does it need my Google password?

No. Sign-in happens on Google's own page, and you can revoke access any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What about the Google Photos API?

Google no longer lets apps read a whole Photos library, so Google Takeout is the way to get all your photos with their metadata. Takeout exports include everything you need.

Does it show videos and HEIC photos?

HEIC photos are decoded on your computer and shown like any other photo. The timeline shows photos today; videos in your Takeout are counted in the inventory and copied across by the Proton Photos and Apple Photos sync.

Why are the dates wrong after unzipping a Google Takeout?

The file's own timestamp is often the export date; the real capture date and GPS live in the .json sidecar next to each photo. Takeout Photo reads those sidecars, so dates and places are right. See browsing a Takeout as a timeline.

Can I see my Google Photos on my Strava, Komoot or Garmin activities?

Yes. The Activities app matches your Google Photos from the Takeout to each activity by capture time and places every photo along the GPS track ("km 12.3 · 10:42"). See photos on your Strava activities — Komoot and Garmin Connect work the same way.

Does it work with Google Drive or Docs from my Takeout?

Not yet. Today it uses the Google Photos part of your Takeout, plus Location History where your export has it. Support for Google Drive and Google Docs files is coming, and the sync app will add more destinations beyond Proton Photos and Apple Photos.

Can I add someone else's Takeout, like my partner's?

Yes. With the yearly plan you can add contributors (other people's Takeouts, e.g. a partner's), so their photos attach to the trips you shared. Their photos are never synced to your Proton Photos or Apple Photos.

What happens when the yearly plan ends?

You keep the Lifetime licence (the first paid year includes it). Manual imports keep working forever, and importing a newer export still rebuilds your library with your edits kept. The automatic-processing features (folder watcher, incremental imports of newer exports, automatic Apple Photos updates, new contributors) stop while the subscription is inactive; existing contributors stay visible. You can re-subscribe any time.

Does the yearly price go up?

No — it goes down. The first year is billed at the full price (e.g. USD 25), and every renewal after that is automatically 50% off (USD 12.50 per year; EUR 20 → EUR 10, GBP 20 → GBP 10, CHF 20 → CHF 10). Nothing for you to do; it renews at half price until you cancel.

How do I cancel or get a refund?

Cancel any time from the app's "Manage subscription" button; the plan runs to the end of the paid year. For a refund, email hello@takepho.com and we will sort it out.
