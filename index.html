How it works

1 Export with Google Takeout

Export your Google Photos with Google Takeout to your computer or Google Drive. This gives you a complete archive of your photos and metadata.

2 Point Takeout Photo at the archives

Point Takeout Photo at the archives — a folder on disk, a Drive link, or sign in with Google and pick the archive files. The app reads them directly.

3 Browse your timeline

Browse your timeline: home periods, day trips and holidays, each with a map and an AI-curated preview. Everything stays on your computer.

What you can do with your Google Photos Takeout

Three things Takeout Photo does with the export you already have — everything stays on your own computer.

Browse it as a timeline

Read the .zip/.tgz archives in place and browse your photos as trips and places on a map, with the real dates and GPS from the JSON sidecars.

See photos on your Strava activities

Match your Google Photos to each Strava activity by capture time and see every photo placed along the GPS track.

Move it to Proton Photos

Import the Google Photos part of your Takeout into Proton Photos in original quality, keeping albums and writing dates and locations into the files.

Features

Automatic trips

Detects where you live, when you moved, and every trip away — so you can browse your life by journey, not just by date.

Places named

Towns and countries are identified from your photos' GPS coordinates. No manual tagging needed.

AI curation

Groups near-duplicates and picks the best photos, on your own machine. Your memories, curated without the cloud.

Maps and routes

See the path you took on each trip. Routes are drawn on an interactive map using open street data.

Works with huge libraries

Reads the archives in place and handles hundreds of GB — no need to unpack them first.

New exports merge in

With the yearly plan, scheduled Takeout exports are picked up and merged automatically — incrementally, without duplicating photos you already have. With Lifetime you re-import a newer export manually and your edits are kept.

Updates

Every release is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Updates are free for everyone, no plan needed. Release notes on GitHub.

Private by design

Everything is processed locally on your computer; your photos are never uploaded to our servers. Google access is read-only and limited to the files you pick.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a web app?

No — it's a desktop app for Mac. A Windows version is planned. Your photos never leave your machine.

Which Macs does it run on?

Apple silicon (arm64) Macs running macOS 12 (Monterey) or later. The app is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

Do I need an account?

No — the app is free to download and explore (the sample library in the app, or the web demo). Importing your own Takeout needs a plan: sign in with Google or Apple at account.takepho.com from the app (Settings → Account). One account covers 2 Macs.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The app keeps a signed entitlement on your Mac and refreshes it about weekly, so it needs internet at least once a month.

Does it need my Google password?

No — sign-in happens on Google's own page, and you can revoke access any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What about the Google Photos API?

Google no longer lets apps read a whole Photos library, so Google Takeout is the way to get all your photos with their metadata. Takeout exports include everything you need.

Why are the dates wrong after unzipping a Google Takeout?

The file's own timestamp is often the export date; the real capture date and GPS live in the .json sidecar next to each photo. Takeout Photo reads those sidecars, so dates and places are right. See browsing a Takeout as a timeline.

Can I see my Google Photos on my Strava activities?

Yes — the Strava app matches your Google Photos from the Takeout to each activity by capture time and places every photo along the GPS track ("km 12.3 · 10:42"). See photos on your Strava activities.

How do I move my Google Photos to Proton Photos?

The sync app moves the Google Photos part of your Takeout into Proton Photos in full original quality, keeping albums (or making trip albums) and writing dates, GPS and descriptions from the JSON sidecars into the files first. See moving Google Photos to Proton Photos.

Can I add someone else's Takeout, like my partner's?

Yes — with the yearly plan you can add contributors (other people's Takeouts, e.g. a partner's), so their photos attach to the trips you shared.

What happens when the yearly plan ends?

You keep the Lifetime licence — the first paid year includes it. Manual imports keep working forever, and importing a newer export still rebuilds your library with your edits kept. The automatic-processing features (folder watcher, incremental imports of newer exports, new contributors) stop while the subscription is inactive; existing contributors stay visible. You can re-subscribe any time.

How do I cancel or get a refund?

Cancel any time in Link's subscription portal — the app's "Manage subscription" button opens it — and the plan runs to the end of the paid year. Payments and refunds are handled by Link (Stripe's merchant of record): contact us and we'll sort it out.
